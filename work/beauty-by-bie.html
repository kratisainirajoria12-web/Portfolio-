02 — CRO case study

Beauty by Bie

Finding where shoppers hesitated on a beauty storefront — and redesigning the product and checkout journey to remove that friction.

Role
UX & CRO designer
Scope
Audit, research, redesign, test plan
Team
Founder, developer, me
Platform
Shopify storefront
★★★★★
Add to bag

Problem

Healthy traffic, but too few visitors were adding to bag, and too many carts were being abandoned before payment.

What I did

Ran a CRO audit combining analytics, heatmaps, recordings and a quick survey, then prioritised and redesigned the key pages.

Outcome

A clear, evidence-backed roadmap and a redesigned product page and cart built to answer shoppers’ questions up front.

01

The problem

Beauty by Bie had a loyal audience on social media and plenty of visits to its store — but that attention wasn’t turning into orders. Visitors browsed, opened product pages and then left.

The brief was open: “find out why, and fix it.” Rather than jumping to a redesign, I started by finding exactly where in the journey people hesitated, and why.

02

My role

I led the audit and redesign: setting up the research, analysing data, synthesising findings, prioritising opportunities with the founder, designing the new pages and writing the test plan for the developer.

  • Heuristic audit
  • Analytics
  • Heatmaps & recordings
  • On-site survey
  • Prioritisation (ICE)
  • UI design
  • A/B test plan

03

Research

Funnel analytics

Mapped drop-off from landing to product page, bag, checkout and purchase.

Heatmaps & recordings

Watched how people actually scrolled, tapped and hesitated on mobile.

Exit survey

Asked leaving visitors one question: what stopped you today?

Heuristic review

Audited every step against e-commerce UX best practice.

04

What we learned

1

Shade was the real decision

Shoppers tapped repeatedly on product images trying to see shades. The shade picker was a small dropdown that many never opened.

“I can’t tell what colour this is going to be on me.”
2

Key answers lived below the fold

Price context, reviews and delivery information sat far down the page — most mobile visitors never scrolled that far.

3

Surprise costs at checkout

Shipping costs appeared only at the last step, which lined up with a sharp spike in abandonment.

“Delivery was more than I expected.”

05

Design decisions

Make shade the hero

Visual swatches replace the dropdown, with the selected shade named and reflected in the product imagery.

Why: Turns the most important decision into the most visible one.

Answer questions above the fold

Price, rating, shade, add-to-bag and delivery promise now sit in the first screen on mobile, with a sticky add-to-bag as you scroll.

Why: Reduces the scrolling and guessing needed before committing.

No surprises in the bag

A free-delivery progress bar and shipping estimate appear in the bag, long before payment.

Why: Removes the shock that was driving late-stage abandonment.

08

Reflection

The biggest lesson: most of the friction wasn’t visual at all — it was missing information. Next, I’d run the planned A/B tests one change at a time so we can attribute results clearly, and extend the shade work into a quick “find my shade” quiz.
