03 — Brand & digital

Healthfab

A rebrand carried through packaging and a new e-commerce experience — one warmer, clearer identity from shelf to screen.

Role
Brand & UI designer
Scope
Identity, packaging, website
Team
Founders, printer, developer, me
Deliverables

Problem

The brand looked like every other discount wellness store, which made it hard to build trust or charge a fair price.

What I did

Defined a new positioning with the founders, then designed the identity, packaging system and e-commerce site around it.

Outcome

A cohesive, ownable brand that works on a shelf, in a feed and on a product page — and a system the team can extend.

01

The problem

Healthfab made genuinely good everyday wellness products, but the brand didn’t say so. A generic logo, bright stock greens and constant sale banners made it look like a discount reseller.

The founders wanted a brand that felt caring and credible — something people would trust enough to make part of a daily routine, without it feeling clinical.

02

My role

I led the rebrand from positioning workshop to launch: brand strategy with the founders, identity design, the packaging system, and the UI design of the new store, including design handover to the developer and print checks with the packaging supplier.

03

Research

Founder workshop

Defined who Healthfab is for, what it stands for and how it should feel.

Customer conversations

Asked existing customers why they bought and what made them hesitate.

Shelf & category audit

Mapped competitor brands to find where the category looked the same.

Moodboarding

Explored three directions before committing to one.

04

What we learned

1

The category shouts

Competitors leaned on bright greens, bold claims and discounts. Calm and warm was an open space.

2

Customers bought for the routine

People talked about habits and small rituals, not ingredients or price.

“It’s the one thing I do for myself every morning.”
3

Trust came from clarity

Hesitation came from unclear usage and ingredient information, not from price.

05

Design decisions

A softer, warmer identity

A rounded serif wordmark, an earthy olive-and-apricot palette and generous whitespace replace the generic green sans.

Why: Signals care and quality, and stands apart on a crowded shelf.

A modular packaging system

One structure, with colour bands and a consistent information hierarchy for every product line.

Why: Easy to extend to new SKUs, and scannable at a glance.

A store built around rituals

Shopping by routine (morning, recovery, sleep) sits alongside categories, with clear usage information on every product page.

Why: Mirrors how customers actually talk about the products.
