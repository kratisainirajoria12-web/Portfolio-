01 · Strengthening Visual Hierarchy

One message, one proof point, one action.

Why it mattersA clear hierarchy lets users scan the first screen along natural Z-pattern movement and locate the proposition before they decide to scroll.
Intended effectDesigned to reduce cognitive load on entry and create a clearer path toward product discovery from the first screen.
02 · Making Brand USPs Visible

Stating the proposition where attention already is.

Why it mattersA concise strip lets users understand why to consider the brand with minimal effort, and supports confidence before any product decision.
Intended effectExpected to reduce the effort needed to understand the brand proposition and to improve information scanning on the way into the catalogue.

Original PDP · observations

Product Page Audit

What’s missing?

OrientationNo breadcrumb trail, so users cannot see where they are or step back to the category they came from.
Product imageryA single swipeable image with dot indicators, no zoom and no thumbnail gallery, so additional views are easy to miss.
EvaluationNo ratings, no review count, no product USPs. Benefits are buried in a paragraph behind a Read More.
ValueA single price with no MRP or discount, and no visible offers at the point of decision.
Purchase actionNo prominent Add to Cart in view, and nothing persistent as the user scrolls the page.
ConfidenceNo delivery estimate, no shipping or returns information, and no way to save or share the product.

Giving the page everything a purchase decision needs, in the order it is needed.

The redesigned PDP in full, split across two panels, with all eleven changes marked in place, each tied to the observation that prompted it.

08 09 10 11
01Breadcrumbs. Shows current location and enables easy backtracking to related pages.
02Zoom interaction on imagery. Lets users inspect detail closely, reducing purchase uncertainty.
03Thumbnail product gallery. Makes multiple product views discoverable rather than hidden in a swipe.
04Wishlist & share actions. Supports engagement beyond the immediate purchase journey.
05Product USP chips. Key benefits become scannable instead of buried in body copy.
06Ratings & review count. Social proof builds credibility and supports an informed decision.
07Price, struck-through MRP, discount. Makes the value of the purchase immediately legible.
08Sticky Add to Cart CTA. Keeps the primary action accessible at every scroll position.
09Estimated delivery checker. Sets delivery expectations upfront, reducing uncertainty at purchase.
10Shipping & returns highlighted. Free shipping, easy returns and refunds reduce purchase hesitation.
11Available offers near the CTA. Promotional benefits are discoverable at the moment of purchase.
Cart Drawer Audit

A cart that totals the order, but never argues for it.

No incentive signalNothing tells the user how close they are to free shipping or an offer threshold.
No cross-sellThe empty space between items and CTA carries no complementary product suggestions.
No trust markersNo secure-payment indication at the point where payment details are about to be requested.
No value summaryA subtotal alone; the discount already earned on the order is never stated.
Cart Drawer · Finding → Design Response

Progress, cross-sell, savings, security.

Four additions to the drawer, each one working on the checkout decision.

01
Progress bar toward free shipping or offerClearly communicates how close the user is to qualifying for free shipping or a 3% discount, making the threshold actionable rather than invisible.
02
Frequently bought togetherSuggests complementary products at the point of review, encouraging additional purchases and supporting a higher average order value.
03
Total savings messageA stated “You saved ₹200 on this order” lets users see the value of the basket instantly and reinforces the benefit of the discounts applied.
04
Secure payment markersRecognisable payment options build trust, reduce concerns about fraud and support completion of the transaction.
